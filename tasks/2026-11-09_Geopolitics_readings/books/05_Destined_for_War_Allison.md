# Review: *Destined for War: Can America and China Escape Thucydides's Trap?* by Graham Allison (2017)

## Overview
Allison, a Harvard political scientist, uses a phrase from Thucydides, the ancient historian of the Peloponnesian War, to describe a pattern: when a rising power threatens to displace an established one, the structural stress can make war likely. He applies it to the US and China.

## Main ideas
- **Thucydides's Trap.** The danger comes from the rising power's growing claims and the ruling power's fear, and small incidents can trigger a larger conflict.
- **Historical cases.** He reviews about 16 cases over the last 500 years, of which about 12 ended in war and 4 did not.
- **China's rise.** He argues China's economic and military growth fits the pattern, and that flashpoints (Taiwan, South China Sea, trade, technology) could escalate.
- **Avoiding war.** The four peaceful cases show that war is not inevitable. He proposes ways to manage rivalry: clear priorities, communication, and understanding the other's core interests.

## Strengths
Accessible, and it organises historical cases into a useful lens. It strongly influenced policy discussion on US–China relations.

## Weaknesses
Critics say the case selection is subjective and the sample is small, that "structure" may be given too much weight compared with leaders and domestic politics, and that some cases are loosely defined.

## Relevance to geopolitics
Central for power-transition theory and for current US–China debates. It also connects to Kissinger's *On China*, which explains how China sees these questions.
