# Review: *On China* by Henry Kissinger (2011)

## Overview
A long study of Chinese diplomacy and strategic thinking, from imperial times to the modern era, by a statesman who helped open relations between the US and China in the early 1970s. Kissinger combines history with personal memories of meetings with Chinese leaders.

## Main ideas
- **Historical self-image.** China long saw itself as the central civilisation, and this shapes its sense of dignity and its approach to foreign relations.
- **Strategic style.** He contrasts Western chess, which aims for decisive battle, with the Chinese game of *wei qi* (go), which is about gradual encirclement and relative advantage. He links this to Sun Tzu's emphasis on patience and psychology.
- **The 1970s opening.** The rapprochement under Nixon and Mao came from shared interests, mainly balancing the Soviet Union.
- **Future relations.** Kissinger argues that neither side should aim to dominate. He advocates a cooperative relationship, a kind of "co-evolution", while managing the risk of rivalry.

## Strengths
A rare blend of history and insider insight, which gives a deeper picture than headline events. It explains Chinese perspectives for Western readers.

## Weaknesses
It reflects the author's own role and his realist view, and gives less space to domestic politics, human rights and the views of ordinary Chinese. The picture of a consistent "Chinese strategic culture" can be criticised as oversimplified.

## Relevance to geopolitics
Essential background on China's approach to diplomacy. It pairs with Allison's book (the structural risk) and with *The Art of War* (the strategic tradition Kissinger refers to).
