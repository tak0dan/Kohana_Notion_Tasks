# Review: *Le labyrinthe des égarés* by Amin Maalouf (2023, French)

## Overview
An essay by the Franco-Lebanese writer Amin Maalouf, subtitled *L'Occident et ses adversaires*. It looks at how the West lost influence and legitimacy in the world, and how its rivals and critics have gained ground. The book is available only in French.

## Main ideas
- **Moral credibility.** Maalouf argues that the West's inconsistency, when it applies principles selectively, has damaged its authority, and he looks at turning points such as the 1956 Suez crisis.
- **Rise of rivals.** He considers how other powers and movements exploit this loss of credibility.
- **Being lost.** The "labyrinth" in the title describes a world in which actors have lost direction, with fear, identity and resentment steering policy.
- **A call for coherence.** He calls for a more consistent and humane approach based on shared values.

## Strengths
Literary and humane, by an author with a double perspective (Middle East and Europe). It offers a view from outside the strictly strategic canon.

## Weaknesses
It is an essay, not a systematic analysis, with less evidence and a more normative tone than the academic books on this list. The argument may feel partly impressionistic.

## Relevance to geopolitics
It adds a cultural and moral dimension to power politics and is a good counterpoint to realists like Walt and Kissinger.
