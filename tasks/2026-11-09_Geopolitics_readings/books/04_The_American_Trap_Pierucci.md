# Review: *The American Trap* by Frédéric Pierucci with Matthieu Aron (English edition 2019)

## Overview
A first-person account by a former senior executive of the French industrial group Alstom. Pierucci was arrested at a New York airport in 2013 in a US corruption investigation (under the Foreign Corrupt Practices Act) and spent time in US detention, while his employer negotiated a settlement. In 2014 Alstom paid a very large fine, and in 2015 its energy business was sold to General Electric.

## Main ideas
- **Law as a weapon.** The authors argue that the US uses its legal system and the reach of its currency and financial system (the dollar, US banks, extraterritorial jurisdiction) as a tool of economic competition.
- **Pressure on individuals.** Prosecutors can pressure executives personally to push companies into settlements and guilty pleas.
- **Economic security.** European states and firms are poorly protected, and the book urges stronger national and EU defences against extraterritorial laws.

## Strengths
Vivid and personal, and it makes an abstract concept ("economic warfare") concrete. It raised a real policy debate in France about the Alstom sale and foreign judicial reach.

## Weaknesses
It is one person's account and necessarily one-sided. Alstom's conduct is part of the story, and the book gives limited weight to the argument that anti-corruption enforcement is legitimate. Readers should check claims against independent reporting.

## Relevance to geopolitics
It illustrates geoeconomics: how sanctions, dollar dominance and legal jurisdiction act as instruments of power, and links to current debates on sanctions and tech controls. Compared with Sun Tzu, it shows "winning without fighting" through economic and legal means.

*Note: the cover credit is often spelled "Pierrucci"; the author's name is Frédéric Pierucci.*
