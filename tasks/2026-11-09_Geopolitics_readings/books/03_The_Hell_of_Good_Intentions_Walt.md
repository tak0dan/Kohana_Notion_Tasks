# Review: *The Hell of Good Intentions* by Stephen M. Walt (2018)

## Overview
A critique of US foreign policy since the end of the Cold War by a leading realist scholar at Harvard. Walt argues that Washington pursued an overambitious strategy, **liberal hegemony**, and that this contributed to a decline in US primacy.

## Main ideas
- **Liberal hegemony.** After 1991, successive administrations of both parties tried to spread democracy, expand alliances and reshape other states, instead of focusing on core interests.
- **The "Blob".** Walt describes a bipartisan foreign-policy establishment (officials, think tanks, commentators) that shares assumptions and seldom pays a price for failure.
- **Costly results.** He points to the Iraq war, the long Afghanistan commitment, NATO expansion and the unsuccessful handling of the Middle East peace process as outcomes of this approach.
- **Alternative: offshore balancing.** The US should keep its power for key regions, rely more on regional allies to balance threats, and avoid open-ended interventions.

## Strengths
Clearly argued, with a strong case that elites rarely face accountability for strategic mistakes. It shaped the debate on restraint in US policy.

## Weaknesses
Critics say Walt underestimates the benefits of the US-led order (stability, trade, alliances) and the risk that withdrawal could create power vacuums. The alternative is described in less detail than the critique.

## Relevance to geopolitics
It is a good example of realist analysis applied to a great power's own behaviour, and a useful contrast to the liberal-internationalist view. It links to Allison's book through the question of how the US should handle China's rise.
